Best 3D Printing Services in Minneapolis (2026)

7 min read Minneapolis–St. Paul, MN

The Twin Cities metro is home to the largest concentration of medical device companies in the world — Medtronic, Abbott (formerly St. Jude Medical), Boston Scientific, and hundreds of their suppliers all operate here. That single fact makes Minneapolis one of the most technically demanding and sophisticated 3D printing markets in the United States, with service bureaus calibrated to the precision, traceability, and compliance requirements of the medical device industry.

This guide covers the Minneapolis–St. Paul 3D printing market, where services are located, and what to look for based on your application.

The Twin Cities 3D Printing Landscape

3D printing services in the metro cluster around major industry centers:

  • Plymouth / Minnetonka — Medtronic headquarters zone, medical device prototyping and production
  • Arden Hills / Shoreview — Abbott and defense (Alliant Techsystems legacy), precision manufacturing
  • Eden Prairie / Hopkins — medical device and industrial engineering corridor
  • Minneapolis Warehouse District / North Loop — design studios, maker culture, startup prototyping
  • St. Paul / East Metro — manufacturing, aerospace, and engineering job shops

FDM Printing in Minneapolis

FDM services are available throughout the metro. Downtown Minneapolis shops serve the design, architecture, and startup ecosystem with fast-turnaround prototyping. Suburban industrial shops — especially in the Plymouth–Eden Prairie corridor — are oriented toward engineering-grade materials and tight tolerances. High-temperature filaments, PEEK, and carbon-fiber-filled materials are more readily available here than in most Midwest cities, driven by medical device R&D demand.

Minnesota's winters also create unique demand for ASA and weather-resistant materials — shops with outdoor and agricultural equipment clients keep these materials in regular stock.

SLA / Resin Printing in Minneapolis

High-precision SLA is in strong demand throughout the Twin Cities, driven by the medical device industry's need for detailed surgical guides, implant prototypes, and device housings. Several shops near the Plymouth and Eden Prairie corridors stock biocompatible resins and have workflows calibrated for regulatory submission requirements. Dental applications are also a strong market, with multiple shops offering castable and model resins for DSO and independent practice clients.

Medical Device Capital

The Twin Cities metro has more medical device companies per capita than anywhere else in the world. If your project involves medical applications, Minneapolis-area shops are among the most experienced in the country for biocompatible materials, ISO 13485 workflows, and FDA submission support.

SLS and Industrial Printing in Minneapolis

SLS nylon and MJF are well-represented in the Twin Cities, primarily in the suburban industrial corridors. Shops in Eden Prairie, Plymouth, and Arden Hills regularly serve medical device clients with production-quality nylon parts requiring post-processing including media blasting and dyeing. Several shops operate certified quality management systems and provide full material lot documentation — capabilities demanded by their medical device clients and available to all customers.

Metal 3D Printing in Minneapolis

Metal additive manufacturing in the Twin Cities is primarily concentrated in the northeast suburbs — Arden Hills, Blaine, and the St. Paul east metro — where precision machining shops have added DMLS and binder-jetting capabilities. Titanium and cobalt-chrome are common materials, driven by orthopedic and cardiovascular device applications. Post-machining, HIP, and material certification are typically available in the same facilities.

Medical device?

Plymouth, Eden Prairie, and Minnetonka shops specialize in biocompatible, traceable, and ISO-compliant workflows — among the best in the US

Design / startup?

North Loop and Warehouse District shops serve Minneapolis's growing tech and design community with fast FDM and resin turnarounds

University and Makerspace Access

The University of Minnesota's College of Science and Engineering has well-equipped fabrication facilities, and the U's medical school maintains device prototyping resources. Twin Cities Maker is a community makerspace in Minneapolis with member access to FDM printers. The Minneapolis Central Library makerspace and several suburban library systems offer community 3D printing access at low cost.

Tips for Ordering 3D Prints in Minneapolis

  • Medical device lead times: Shops serving the device industry operate on fast design-iteration cycles. Many offer 24–48 hour turnaround for FDM prototypes — ask explicitly if you need it.
  • Winter shipping: Minneapolis winters can affect shipping from out-of-state bureaus. For time-sensitive jobs, local shops with pickup options are worth a slight price premium in January–February.
  • ISO documentation: Many local shops include material certifications and inspection reports as standard practice — don't assume you need to ask for them separately.
  • Quality bar is high: Because local shops primarily serve demanding medical device clients, their quality standards often exceed what you'd find at general-purpose bureaus in other cities.
