Best 3D Printing Services in Philadelphia (2026)

7 min read Philadelphia, PA

Philadelphia's combination of world-class research universities, a major biotech and pharmaceutical corridor, and a long manufacturing history makes it one of the Northeast's most capable 3D printing markets. From University City's research labs to King of Prussia's aerospace and defense suppliers, the Philadelphia metro offers professional additive manufacturing services for nearly every application.

This guide covers where 3D printing services are concentrated in Philly, what industries they serve, and how to find the right shop for your project.

The Philadelphia 3D Printing Landscape

Philadelphia's 3D printing services cluster around the city's major industry centers:

  • University City (West Philadelphia) — UPenn, Drexel, and CHOP-adjacent research and medical prototyping
  • Center City / Old City — product design firms, architecture studios, marketing prototype shops
  • Navy Yard — advanced manufacturing, defense, and industrial fabrication
  • King of Prussia / Route 202 Corridor — aerospace, defense contractors, pharmaceutical and biotech manufacturing
  • Northeast Philadelphia / Bucks County — manufacturing and engineering job shops

FDM Printing in Philadelphia

FDM services are available throughout the metro, from downtown design-focused shops serving Center City architects and product developers to suburban industrial shops in the King of Prussia corridor handling engineering-grade materials. Shops near the Navy Yard serve defense and marine applications with specialty materials including ASA for UV resistance and high-temperature filaments for industrial jigs and fixtures.

For large-format architectural models — a strong market given Philadelphia's active urban development — several shops offer build volumes large enough for multi-story building section models at a scale useful for presentation.

SLA / Resin Printing in Philadelphia

Philadelphia's biotech and pharmaceutical industry creates sustained demand for high-precision resin printing. University City shops serve the research market with biocompatible resins and castable materials for dental and medical device prototyping. Several shops near Jefferson Hospital and Penn Medicine are positioned specifically to serve clinical research and device development timelines.

Biotech and Pharma Hub

The Philadelphia region hosts one of the largest concentrations of pharmaceutical and biotech companies in the world, centered on the Route 202 corridor and University City. If you need FDA-compliant or biocompatible 3D printed parts, look for shops with documented medical device experience in these corridors.

SLS and Industrial Printing in Philadelphia

SLS nylon and MJF services in the Philadelphia area are primarily concentrated in the suburban corridors — King of Prussia, Malvern, and the Route 30 Mainline corridor. These shops serve the aerospace, defense, and medical device sectors with production-capable equipment and quality management systems. Lockheed Martin's presence in the region, along with a dense cluster of defense subcontractors, drives demand for AS9100-certified additive manufacturing with full material traceability.

Metal 3D Printing in Philadelphia

Metal additive manufacturing in the Philadelphia area is handled by specialized shops primarily in the King of Prussia and Conshohocken areas, where existing precision machining infrastructure supports hybrid additive-subtractive workflows. Titanium, stainless steel, and Inconel are common materials, driven by aerospace and medical device demand. Several shops offer certified workflows with full material lot traceability for regulatory submissions.

Biotech / Medical?

University City and King of Prussia shops specialize in biocompatible and FDA-compliant workflows for research and device development

Architecture / Design?

Center City and Old City shops serve Philadelphia's active architecture and product design community with fast-turnaround FDM and resin

University and Makerspace Access

Philadelphia has exceptional university fabrication resources. The University of Pennsylvania's Pennovation Center and Drexel University's ExCITe Center both have fabrication labs with 3D printing available to affiliated researchers and students. Temple University's engineering school and Jefferson University's biomedical engineering program round out the academic access points. The Philadelphia area also has several community makerspaces including Hive76, which offers member access to FDM printers at affordable rates.

Tips for Ordering 3D Prints in Philadelphia

  • Pharma calendar: Shops near the Route 202 corridor get heavy demand during FDA submission seasons. Build lead time buffer into regulated projects.
  • University partnerships: If you're affiliated with UPenn, Drexel, or Jefferson, check internal fabrication resources before going commercial — costs can be significantly lower for affiliated users.
  • Navy Yard access: Some Navy Yard shops require facility access clearance. Confirm logistics before choosing a shop in that district.
  • Suburban vs. city pricing: King of Prussia industrial shops typically offer lower per-part pricing than Center City shops — worthwhile if you don't need same-day pickup.
